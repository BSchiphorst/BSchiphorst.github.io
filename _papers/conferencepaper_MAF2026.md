Abstract: Concentration risk in credit portfolios refers to the potential for a single exposure or group of exposures to produce losses large enough to threaten the financial institution. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) is commonly used to measure name concentration, but fails to capture risk arising from obligor-to-obligor network dependence, such as in supply chains or financial networks. While complex models exist for quantifying the impact of higher-order network dependence on portfolio losses, practitioners require simpler methods to assess materiality a priori. We therefore propose a model-free Network-based HHI (NHHI) to quantify concentration risk using exposure shares and network weights. We provide an intuitive theoretical motivation and showcase several attractive properties, including convenient \textquote{rule-of-thumb} materiality thresholds for risk managers. Simulation-based numerical results verify that our simple approach provides a robust quantification of concentration risk for various network structures and connection strengths.
